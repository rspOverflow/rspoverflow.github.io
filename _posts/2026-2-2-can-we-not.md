---
title: "Can we not? Why replacing humans with LLMs is a bad idea."
categories:
  - Opinion
---

Within the tech world, there is a constant chase for efficiency. There is nothing wrong with being efficient of course, it's simply a good business 
practice. However, there are right ways of becoming more efficient and wrong (risky) ways of becoming efficient. The wrong approach can expose a business 
to legal and economic risk. In my opinion, as a business decision, the decision to replace technical employees (like programmers) with LLM 
agents is a bad idea even from an investor's perspective.

## Accountability

First, firing human programmers and replacing them with LLM agents will lead to unquantifiable and unpredictable legal risk. Within companies, 
accountability is traditionally a traceable chain. From junior engineers who write the code, to senior engineers who review the code and give it 
permission to be merged, to the QA engineers who look at the code and see if it's good enough to be pushed to production, and finally to the CTO who 
will ultimately answer to the board as to why the service crashed at 3 AM. In every step of the process, a human can be held accountable and changes can 
be made.

LLM agents break this delicate chain of accountability. By directly using LLM agents to write applications without humans in the loop, who is held 
responsible for say, a security breach? Are the LLM providers going to be held liable? No, they just run servers, they didn't do anything. Can the LLM 
itself be held liable? No, it's not human. Without a strict accountability chain, executives, founders, and the company itself is vulnerable to unbounded 
legal risk. As an investor, this should concern you, unbounded legal risk means that at any moment your investment could find itself in a lot of legal 
hot water and burning through cash trying to pay for lawyers before ultimately going bankrupt. Insurers may not be able to accurately or even be willing 
to insure for any damages (since they assume some human accountability) for the business. By removing humans from the loop, you will find 
yourself with a very thin legal shield should a black swan event (like a security breach) occur.

## Market and Innovation

Beyond the accountability issues, there is a fundamental commodity argument to the problem as well. In any industry in general, you want to have a 
"moat". This "moat" gives your business an edge over your competitors like a proprietary technology. The proprietary technology or ideas used to create 
these moats originate from specialized and experienced human employees. LLM agents simply cannot replicate the same creative thought process. LLM agents 
simply only know the most likely solution to a problem not the most optimal. A business that replaces its human engineers with LLM agents simply trade 
their creative engines for a statistically "most likely" engine based on past data. For certain sensitive applications like high frequency trading, this 
past looking behavior and synthesizing solutions from old algorithms or heuristics is not ideal and can cause the trader to lose a lot of money to a 
competitor who continues to innovate. The use of LLM agents also call into question the concept of IP (Intellectual Property) since LLM agents (in 
reality) regurgitate code with minor modifications rather than developing unique code making copyright questionable. For a business, these issues opens 
up a lot of legal risk.

Furthermore, the extensive use of LLM agents to develop products within the industry will cause products to become extremely similar with little to no 
unique value. This leads into the concept of "enshittification" where products plateau in innovation (a.k.a become "slop") and value while prices may 
continue to increase. This causes consumers to become cynical to the state of the industry and the product. This causes them to leave, to seek other 
alternatives. This behavior directly hurts a business by reducing its income and customer base. As an investor, the income and customer base of a 
business is an important cornerstone of its valuation and your ROI.

## Conclusion

It is a bad business decision to replace human employees with LLM agents due to the unquantifiable risks involved and ruining the consumer perception of 
the industry or product. As a investor, it is in your best interest that a business continues to produce a valuable product and have a loyal consumer 
base who still perceives value within your product. LLM agents destroy this as they are optimized to generate the most likely solutions, not the most 
valuable solutions for your customers. Thus, it is not wise to replace human employees with LLM agents entirely and have an entire division or even the 
whole company operated by LLM agents. I'm not completely against LLM technology, but it begs the following: Assuming innovation cannot be undone, is 
there a way to combine LLM agents and human programmers together to get the best of both worlds?
